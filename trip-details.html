Everest Base Camp Trek Overview

The Everest Base Camp Trek immerses you in the breathtaking landscapes and rich Sherpa heritage of the Everest region in northeast Nepal. This journey leads you to the legendary Everest Base Camp at 5,364 m / 17,598 ft, located in the Solukhumbu District. The highest point of the trek is Kala Patthar at 5,555 m / 18,208 ft, which brings you face-to-face with mighty Mount Everest.

The 14-Day journey, guided by local experienced Sherpa guides, begins with a scenic 40-minute flight from Kathmandu to Lukla (2,860 m / 9,350 ft). Depending on the season, the trek may start from Manthali: a 5-hour drive from Kathmandu, followed by a 20-minute flight to Lukla.

From Lukla, the trail follows the Dudh Koshi River, passing through charming villages like Phakding (2,650 m / 8,562 ft). Along the way, you pass through Rimshuing Monastery and cross the Hillary suspension bridge, an early highlight of the trek in Sagarmatha National Park.

You move through the rhododendron forests and alpine meadows before reaching Namche Bazaar (3,440 m / 11,285 ft). This lively Sherpa town is the gateway to Everest, where you spend a day for acclimatization. You can explore local markets, Khumjung village, and Syangboche airport, discover Sherpa culture at the museum, and observe the yaks grazing in the farms.

Lukla Flight Information

During peak trekking seasons (March–May and October–November), Lukla flights operate from Manthali (Ramechhap) instead of Kathmandu due to air traffic congestion. On these days, you will leave Kathmandu around 12:30 a.m. for a 5 – 6 hour drive to Manthali to catch your early morning flight.

During off-peak months, flights usually depart directly from Kathmandu.

Flight delays and cancellations are common due to changing mountain weather, so we strongly recommend adding at least two buffer days after your trek. These buffer days help prevent delays or disruptions to your international travel plans.

As we answer your queries, we will guide you on physical and mental preparation for the trek. Our goal is to clear all your doubts and help you feel confident, ready, and excited for the trek.

Where Will You Stay During the Everest Base Camp Trek?

During the trek, you will stay in locally run teahouses, offering a warm, welcoming environment.

Accommodation for the Everest Base Camp Trek includes a mix of comfortable lower-altitude lodges and simpler high-altitude teahouses, designed to help you rest and acclimatize.

Each stop offers a distinct stay, from comfortable rooms with private bathrooms to basic lodges that reflect the raw beauty of the Himalayas.

As you follow the classic route, your stay includes:

  • Total Nights: 13 nights
  • 6 nights in lower-altitude villages:
    • Lukla: 1 night
    • Phakding: 2 nights
    • Namche Bazaar: 3 nights
    • Accommodation: Rooms with private, attached bathrooms, twin beds, pillows, and blankets
  • 7 nights in higher-altitude villages:
    • Tengboche: 2 nights
    • Dingboche: 2 nights
    • Lobuche: 1 night
    • Gorak Shep: 1 night
    • Pheriche: 1 night
    • Accommodation: Standard rooms with shared facilities (toilets, limited hot water), cozy and welcoming for high-altitude trekking

Tip: Lodges are more comfortable at lower altitudes and become simpler as you ascend.

What Amenities Are Available in the Rooms?

During the Everest Base Camp trek, teahouse rooms are simple but comfortable, offering essential facilities to help you rest after a long day on the trail.

Amenities may vary by altitude, with better facilities at lower elevations and more basic setups at higher elevations.

Sleeping Arrangements:

  • Twin beds with mattresses, pillows, and blankets.
  • Rooms are basic but comfortable.
  • Use a sleeping bag for extra warmth.
  • Single rooms are rare and usually available only in the off-season (extra ~$5–$10).
  • Sleeping areas are not heated.

Note: We can provide a used sleeping bag for your use if you don't have your own.

Bathrooms and Toilets:

  • Attached bathrooms are available at lower altitudes.
  • Shared toilets (Western-style or squat) at higher elevations.
  • Lukla, Phakding, and Namche Bazaar offer rooms with private bathrooms.
  • Some lodges at lower elevations offer upgraded rooms and better amenities.

Hot Showers:

  • Available at most lodges for an extra cost (~$5–$8 per shower).
  • Cold showers are common at higher altitudes.
  • Dining Areas and Meals:
  • Meals are served in communal dining halls, often the warmest place in the lodge.
  • Great space to relax and socialize with fellow trekkers.

Electricity and Charging:

  • Most tea houses rely on solar power.
  • Charging devices cost around ~$5–$7 per item.
  • Carrying a power bank or portable solar charger is practical.
  • 2-pin charge slots are available. So you may bring a universal adaptor.

Wi-Fi and Connectivity:

  • Wi-Fi is available in some teahouses for an extra fee (~$3–$5).
  • Connection becomes slower and less reliable at higher altitudes.

Water:

  • Bottled water is available at shops and teahouses along the trail at an extra cost.
  • Carry a refilled bottle for boiled or filtered water (~$3 – $5 per liter).
  • Using water purification tablets or a LifeStraw is recommended.

Tip: Don't drink water directly from a river, glacier, tap, or any other untreated water source. Keep in mind that the cost of bottled water will increase as you move higher.

The cost included here is an approximate price. It may vary by season, altitude, and availability.

Meals on the Everest Base Camp Trek are simple, warm, and designed to keep you energized. You will enjoy freshly prepared food at teahouses along the trail, providing you with the nutrition for recovery.

We prioritize your 'Gut Health' by selecting teahouses with strong hygiene standards. Beyond standard meals, you will receive fresh fruit supplements to boost your stamina.

How long is the Everest Base Camp Trek?▾
The classic itinerary takes 12 to 14 days round trip from Lukla, including acclimatization days at Namche Bazaar and Dingboche.
Where is Everest Base Camp?▾
Everest Base Camp sits at roughly 5,364 meters in the Khumbu region of northeastern Nepal, at the foot of the Khumbu Icefall.
What are the age restrictions for the Everest Base Camp Trek?▾
Trekkers are generally required to be at least 10 years old, with no strict upper age limit provided you're in good health and reasonably fit.
How crowded is the Everest Base Camp Trek?▾
Peak seasons (spring and autumn) see steady foot traffic on the main trail, though it rarely feels overcrowded outside of a few busy teahouse stops.
What are the major attractions of the Everest Base Camp Trek?▾
Highlights include Namche Bazaar, Tengboche Monastery, Kala Patthar's sunrise viewpoint, and the base camp itself beneath the Khumbu Icefall.
Is it possible to complete the Everest Base Camp Trek within a short duration?▾
Shorter itineraries using helicopter transfers for part of the route can compress the trip to around 7-9 days, though this reduces acclimatization time.
Can I see Mount Everest from the Everest Base Camp?▾
From base camp itself the summit is largely hidden by surrounding peaks; the classic view of Everest comes from Kala Patthar, a short climb above Gorak Shep.
Can I customize my Everest Base Camp itinerary?▾
Yes, itineraries can be adjusted for extra acclimatization days, side trips, or alternative return routes such as flying out from Syangboche.
What is the total cost of the Everest Base Camp trek?▾
Costs typically range from $1,200 to $2,000 per person depending on group size, season, and the level of service included.

How difficult is the Everest Base Camp trek?▾
It's rated moderate to challenging, mainly due to altitude rather than technical terrain, with long walking days at high elevation.
Is the Everest Base Camp Trek harder than the Manaslu Circuit Trek?▾
Manaslu Circuit is generally considered more physically demanding due to the high Larke Pass crossing, while EBC is longer at altitude overall.
Do I need any technical climbing skills?▾
No technical climbing skills are required; the route is a trekking trail suitable for anyone with reasonable fitness and hiking experience.
How many hours do I walk each day?▾
Most days involve 5 to 7 hours of walking, with shorter acclimatization days built in around Namche and Dingboche.
Is EBC Trek challenging for beginners?▾
Beginners can complete the trek successfully with proper preparation, a gradual pace, and adequate acclimatization time.
Can I trek to EBC alone?▾
Independent trekking is possible, though hiring a licensed guide is recommended for safety, navigation, and support in case of altitude issues.
Is the Everest Base Camp trek suitable for seniors and children?▾
With good fitness and a slower, well-acclimatized pace, both seniors and older children have completed the trek successfully.
Which one should I choose: Everest Base Camp trek or Annapurna Base Camp trek?▾
EBC offers higher altitude and Sherpa culture, while Annapurna Base Camp is shorter, lower elevation, and generally easier.
What is the hardest day of the Everest Base Camp?▾
The push from Gorak Shep to Base Camp and up Kala Patthar is widely considered the toughest day due to altitude and distance combined.
Everest Base Camp vs Annapurna Circuit, which is the better trekking option in Nepal?▾
Annapurna Circuit offers more varied landscapes and villages, while EBC delivers a more concentrated high-mountain and Sherpa cultural experience.

Preparation & Fitness

Expand All
Do I need training for the EBC trek?▾
Training is highly recommended to build the cardiovascular and leg endurance needed for consecutive days of high-altitude walking.
How should I train for the EBC trek?▾
A mix of cardio, hill walking with a loaded pack, and strength training for 2-3 months beforehand works well for most trekkers.
How to prepare to trek to Everest Base Camp?▾
Beyond fitness, prepare by breaking in your boots, packing appropriate layers, and learning the basics of altitude sickness prevention.
How to acclimate to high altitude quickly?▾
Ascend gradually, follow the "climb high, sleep low" principle, stay hydrated, and build rest days into the itinerary at key elevations.
Can I do an EBC trek without training?▾
It's possible for very fit individuals, but lack of preparation increases the risk of fatigue and altitude-related discomfort on the trail.

Permits

Expand All
What permits do I need for the EBC trek?▾
You'll need the Sagarmatha National Park entry permit and the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality permit.
Where can I obtain these permits?▾
Permits can be arranged in Kathmandu or on arrival in Lukla and Monjo, and are usually handled by your trekking agency.
How much do the permits cost?▾
Combined permit costs typically run around $50 per person, though prices can change with local regulations.

Insurance & Visa

Expand All
Do I need travel insurance for the EBC trek?▾
Yes, insurance covering high-altitude trekking and emergency helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 meters is essential.
How do I get a Nepal visa?▾
Most nationalities can obtain a visa on arrival at Kathmandu airport, or apply in advance through a Nepali embassy.

Guides & Porters

Expand All
Is a guide mandatory for the EBC trek?▾
A licensed guide is now required by regulation for trekking in this region and is strongly recommended for safety.
Should I hire a porter as well?▾
A porter carries your main baggage so you only need a day pack, which significantly eases the physical demand of the trek.

Accommodation

Expand All
What type of accommodation is available on the trail?▾
Teahouses provide simple twin rooms along the entire route, with more comfortable lodges available in Namche and Lukla.
Are private rooms available?▾
Most teahouses offer private twin rooms, though at higher altitudes rooms are more basic and shared bathrooms are common.

Food & Drinks

Expand All
What food is available during the trek?▾
Teahouses serve dal bhat, noodles, soups, and basic Western dishes, with menus becoming simpler at higher elevations.
Is the drinking water safe?▾
Boiled or filtered water is available for purchase at teahouses, and a personal filter or purification tablets are recommended as backup.

Weather

Expand All
What is the best season for the EBC trek?▾
Spring (March-May) and autumn (September-November) offer the most stable weather and clearest mountain views.
How cold does it get at Base Camp?▾
Nighttime temperatures near base camp can drop well below freezing, even during the warmer trekking seasons.

Health & Safety

Expand All
What are the symptoms of altitude sickness?▾
Common symptoms include headache, nausea, dizziness, and fatigue; these should never be ignored during the trek.
What safety measures are in place on the trek?▾
Guides monitor for altitude sickness daily, itineraries include acclimatization days, and emergency evacuation plans are always in place.

Culture & Lifestyle

Expand All
What culture will I experience on the trek?▾
The trail passes through Sherpa villages rich in Buddhist tradition, with monasteries, prayer wheels, and mani walls along the way.
Are there any cultural etiquette tips?▾
Walk clockwise around religious structures, ask before photographing locals, and dress modestly when visiting monasteries.
